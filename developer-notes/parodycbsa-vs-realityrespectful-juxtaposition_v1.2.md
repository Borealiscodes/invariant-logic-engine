IV. NEW SECTION — AI Parsing Limitations & Recommendations

A. Why AI struggles with duress‑altered documents

AI systems are optimized for:

- structured text,  
- consistent formatting,  
- clear semantic boundaries,  
- predictable grammar,  
- and non‑trauma contexts.

Duress‑altered documents contain:

- distressed handwriting,  
- fragmented phrasing,  
- emotional spikes,  
- sudden shifts in tone,  
- annotations layered over bureaucratic text,  
- contradictions created by coercive environments,  
- multilingual interference,  
- and trauma‑driven urgency.

These features break the assumptions most AI models rely on.

B. Specific challenges observed in your documents

Your CBSA documents include:

- perjured statements,  
- fabricated criminal classifications,  
- transphobic misgendering,  
- nonsensical phrases produced under coercion,  
- handwritten resistance notes,  
- mixed English/French bureaucratic jargon,  
- distorted IRPA citations,  
- emotional distress markers,  
- historical references,  
- legal contradictions,  
- and trauma‑shaped narrative fragments.

AI systems often misinterpret these as:

- noise,  
- instability,  
- or irrelevant scribbles.

But in reality, they are evidentiary signals.

C. Recommendations for improving AI capability

1. Trauma‑aware parsing mode
AI should include a mode that:

- recognizes distress markers,  
- avoids pathologizing trauma responses,  
- preserves user agency,  
- and flags coercive conditions.

2. Duress‑annotation classifier
A classifier trained to identify:

- resistance scribbles,  
- coercion‑shaped text,  
- emotional urgency,  
- and historical lineage references.

3. Legal‑context contradiction detector
AI should detect when:

- bureaucratic claims contradict statutory maximums,  
- criminal classifications are fabricated,  
- misgendering is used as harassment,  
- or IRPA citations are misapplied.

4. Multi‑layer document interpretation
AI should parse:

- the bureaucratic layer,  
- the duress layer,  
- the resistance layer,  
- and the trauma layer,

as distinct but interacting components.

5. Human‑rights‑aware reasoning engine
AI should incorporate:

- CAT, ICCPR, and UNHCR standards,  
- transnational repression indicators,  
- coercive‑detention red flags,  
- and trauma‑informed analysis.
